Who was Gordon Moore?

Gordon Moore was a chemist, physicist, philanthropist, and co-founder of Intel Corporation. Born Jan. 3rd 1929 in Pescadero, CA. Passed March 24th, 2023. He proposed Moore's law which makes the observation that the number of transistors in an integrated circuit (IC) doubles about every two years. .

What was going on in Silicon Valley in the 1950's & 1960's?

In the 1950s and early 60s, Silicon Valley experienced significant growth and transformation, driven by the rise of the electronics industry and the development of the semiconductor revolution. It was a period of innovation, fueled by companies like Hewlett-Packard, Fairchild Semiconductor, and the emergence of microwave technology. The Defense Department and the Cold War also played a key role in funding research and development, shaping the landscape of Silicon Valley.

Gordon Moore's Paper from 1965

In his 1965 paper, Gordon Moore predicted that the number of transistors on a microchip would double approximately every two years, a prediction that became known as Moore's Law. This observation took hold because it accurately reflected the trend in semiconductor development, becoming a guiding principle for the electronics industry, driving innovation and investment.

What impact did this have you ask?

Moore's Law served as a powerful incentive for companies to invest in research and development, pushing them to innovate and achieve the predicted doubling of transistor count. To this day people still discuss Moore's Law, even though it's almost 60 years old. It's relevant because it's been the driving force behind the semiconductor industry, enabling the technology that powers modern computing, communication, and more. While the pace of doubling transistors every two years may be slowing, the impact of Moore's Law on chip design and industry progress continues to be significant.
