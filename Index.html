A quote from Gordon Moore

"The number of transistors on a microchip doubles about every two years, though the cost of computers is halved."

Gordon Moore was a chemist, physicist, philanthropist, and co-founder of Intel Corporation.

Born Jan. 3rd 1929 in Pescadero, CA. Passed March 24th, 2023.

What Was Moore's Law

Moore's law was a prediction made by Gordon Moore in 1965. Moore stated that the number of transistors in integrated circuits would double approximately every two years. When Moore first spoke these words, it was just an observation about the speed that technology was advancing with semiconductors. However, his words became a driving force for innovation and became a self-fulfilling prophecy. Which helped influence the semiconductor industry and broader technological landscape.

What does the future look like?

Moore’s law stated the number of transistors on a microchip would double approximately every 2 years, it is believed that this may be slowing down although the future of computing is not ending. However, as these transistors continue to shrink in size, the physical limitations of silicon lithography ( a process used to transfer intricate patterns onto silicon wafers ) are being reached. What this means is that traditional methods of shrinking transistors to increase performance are becoming increasingly difficult and expensive to manufacture.

With these limitations fast approaching, the future of computing will involve moving beyond traditional transistor scaling, some options are:

3D integration: Stacking multiple layers of chips together to increase density and performance.

Photonic Computing: Uses light instead of electricity to process information, offering potential advantages in speed and energy efficiency.

Neuromorphic Computing: Mimicking the structure and function of the human brain to create new types of computing systems.

Quantum Computing: Leveraging quantum mechanical phenomena to solve problems that are intractable for classical computers.
